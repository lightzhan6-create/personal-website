Servo Motor Applications

Designed for Servo Motor Transmission

Timing belts provide a positive, synchronous connection between the servo motor and the driven mechanism. The belt teeth engage with pulley teeth to help maintain a fixed transmission relationship without normal belt slip.

  • Servo motor drive systems
  • Linear modules
  • XY positioning systems
  • PCB handling equipment
  • SMT loaders and unloaders
  • Automated conveyors
  • Pick-and-place mechanisms
  • Packaging machinery
  • Robotics and automation equipment
  • Precision transmission mechanisms

Tooth Profile Options

Timing Belt Tooth Profiles

The supplied profile reference includes traditional trapezoidal profiles and curvilinear / arc profiles. Profile selection should match the pulley and original belt specification.

Trapezoidal Timing Belts

T2.5, T5, T10 and T20 profiles are commonly used in traditional synchronous transmission systems.

Curvilinear / Arc Timing Belts

3M, 5M, 8M, 14M and 20M profiles use rounded tooth engagement and are widely used in modern industrial transmission systems.

Replacement Belt Identification

How to Identify Your Existing Timing Belt

A clear, repeatable check helps reduce uncertainty when the original model marking is missing.

Identify the Tooth Profile

Check whether the belt uses a trapezoidal or curvilinear / arc tooth profile.

Count the Teeth

Count the total number of teeth on the existing belt.

Measure the Pitch

Measure from the center of one tooth to the center of the next tooth.

Measure the Belt Length / Circumference

Measure the total belt circumference or confirm the pitch length from the marking.

Measure the Belt Width

Measure the belt width accurately using a caliper whenever possible.

If the original belt marking is still visible, send a clear photo of the printed model number.

FAQ

Questions Before Matching a Belt

Share the original belt information first whenever possible. It is the most direct basis for a replacement review.

Can timing belts be used with servo motors?

Yes. Timing belts are widely used in servo-driven automation systems because the toothed belt and pulley provide synchronized transmission without normal belt slip.

How do I identify my existing timing belt?

Check the printed model first. If the marking is unavailable, identify the tooth profile, pitch, tooth count, belt length and belt width.

What information is required for a quotation?

The belt model, pitch, number of teeth, width, quantity and application photos are recommended.

Can you supply non-standard belt widths?

Please send the required width and application details for evaluation.

Can you help identify a belt from photos?

Yes. Clear photos of the tooth profile, belt marking and overall belt can help with preliminary identification.
